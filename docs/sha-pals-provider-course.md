## Course Overview (pp. 5–7)

### Goal of the Saudi PALS Provider Course
Enhance pediatric patient outcomes by training healthcare personnel to recognize and manage pediatric patients experiencing respiratory crises, shock, or cardiopulmonary arrest.

### Learning Objectives
- Recognize cardiac arrest and start high-quality CPR as soon as possible, according to Saudi Heart Association (SHA) basic life support (BLS) guidelines.
- Recognize the difference between respiratory distress and failure and begin treatment as soon as possible.
- Establish a distinction between compensated and uncompensated (hypotensive) shock and begin early and appropriate management.
- Differentiate between unstable and stable arrhythmia patients and begin treatment as soon as possible.
- Correctly assess, initiate, and lead post–cardiac arrest management.

## Module 1: Systematic Approach to the Critically Ill Child (pp. 8–23)

### Rapid Assessment: Pediatric Assessment Triangle (PAT) (pp. 8–9)
- **Appearance:** Tone, interactiveness, consolability, look/gaze, speech/cry (lethargic, flaccid, limp, or poor muscle tone).
- **Work of Breathing:** Nasal flaring, retractions (intercostal, suprasternal, subcostal), accessory muscle use, head bobbing, abnormal audible breath sounds (stridor, wheezing, grunting), respiratory rate (rapid, slow, or apneic).
- **Circulation to Skin:** Pallor, mottling, cyanosis, or flushing of skin and mucous membranes.

### Initial Resuscitation Check (p. 9)
- **Shout-tap-shout:** Tap shoulder (child) or sole of foot (infant).
- **If unresponsive:** Activate EMS / RRT / Code team; get cardiac monitor / defibrillator / AED.
- **Open airway:** Head tilt–chin lift (or jaw thrust if cervical spine injury suspected).
- **Pulse & breathing check (simultaneous, 5–10 s):**
  - **Infant:** Brachial (or femoral) artery.
  - **Child:** Carotid (or femoral) artery.
  - **Pulseless & apneic (or agonal gasps):** Start high-quality CPR immediately.
  - **Apneic with pulse (≥ 60/min):** Rescue breathing via bag-mask ventilation (BMV) and recheck pulse every 2 minutes.

### Primary Assessment (A-B-C-D-E) (pp. 10–14)
- **Airway:** Assess patency; open with head tilt–chin lift or jaw thrust; suction (no blind finger sweep); OPA (unconscious without gag reflex) or NPA (intact gag reflex, no basilar skull fracture); advanced airway / NIV if unmaintainable.
- **Breathing:** Rate, effort, chest expansion, breath sounds, $\text{SpO}_2$ (maintain $> 94\%$ with supplemental $\text{O}_2$, BMV, NIV, or ETT).
- **Circulation:** Central vs. peripheral pulses, HR/rhythm, BP, skin color & temperature, capillary refill time ($> 2\text{ s}$ is prolonged); establish IV/IO access.
- **Disability:** Neurological status via **AVPU** (Awake, Voice, Painful, Unresponsive), pupillary response (**PERRL**), seizure activity, and point-of-care blood glucose.
- **Exposure:** Full exam for trauma, rashes (petechiae/purpura), burns, edema, and core temperature while preventing hypothermia.

| **Newborn / Neonate (0–28 d)** | 30–60 | 100–200 | 60–85 | 35–55 | $< 60$ |

**Quick Assessment for Hypoglycemia (Table 1.1, p. 12):**
- **Neonates:** Plasma glucose $< 45\text{ mg/dL}$
- **Infants, children, and adolescents:** Plasma glucose $< 60\text{ mg/dL}$

### Secondary Assessment & SAMPLE History (pp. 15–18)
- **S**igns and symptoms
- **A**llergies
- **M**edications (including last dose and recent changes)
- **P**ast medical/surgical history (birth history, immunization status)
- **L**ast meal (type and time of intake / I&Os)
- **E**vents leading to the current episode and treatments already given

### High-Performance Resuscitation Team (6 Roles) (pp. 19–23)
1. **Team Leader:** Assigns roles, makes clinical decisions, oversees CPR quality.
2. **Compressor:** Performs chest compressions, rotates every 2 minutes.
3. **Airway Manager:** Opens airway, provides BVM / advanced airway ventilation.
4. **AED / Defibrillator / CPR Coach:** Operates monitor/defibrillator, coaches compression depth/rate/recoil.
5. **IV/IO Provider:** Establishes vascular access and administers weight-based medications + saline flush.
6. **Time Recorder:** Tracks 2-minute CPR cycles, epinephrine 3–5 min intervals, and logs events; leads structured debriefing after the code.

## Module 2: Recognition and Management of Respiratory Distress and Failure (pp. 25–48)

### Definitions & Categories (pp. 25–26)
- **Respiratory Failure:** $\text{PaO}_2 < 60\text{ mmHg}$ (inadequate oxygenation) or $\text{PaCO}_2 > 50\text{ mmHg}$ (inadequate ventilation); clinical progression from tachypnea/tachycardia/agitation to bradypnea/apnea, bradycardia, cyanosis, and lethargy/unresponsiveness.
- **Four Categories (Table 2.1, p. 26):**
  1. **Upper airway obstruction** (croup, anaphylaxis, foreign body): inspiratory stridor, barking cough, hoarseness.
  2. **Lower airway obstruction** (bronchiolitis, asthma): expiratory wheezing, prolonged expiratory phase, hyperinflation.
  3. **Lung tissue disease** (infectious/aspiration pneumonia, cardiogenic/noncardiogenic pulmonary edema): grunting, crackles, decreased breath sounds, hypoxemia.
  4. **Central respiratory disorders / disordered control of breathing** (elevated ICP / Cushing's triad, neuromuscular disease, drug/opioid overdose): variable/irregular respiratory pattern, shallow effort.

### Airway Equipment, Sizing & Confirmation (pp. 37–48)
- **Oxygen Delivery (pp. 42–43):**
  - Nasal cannula: $0.25\text{–}4\text{ L/min}$ ($\text{FiO}_2\ 22\text{–}60\%$)
  - Simple face mask: $6\text{–}10\text{ L/min}$ ($\text{FiO}_2\ 35\text{–}60\%$)
  - Nonrebreather mask with reservoir: $10\text{–}15\text{ L/min}$ ($\text{FiO}_2\ 90\text{–}95\%$)
- **Bag-Mask Ventilation (BMV) (pp. 44–45):**
  - Pediatric bag ($450\text{–}500\text{ mL}$) at $10\text{–}15\text{ L/min}\ \text{O}_2$; Adult bag ($\ge 1000\text{ mL}$) at $\ge 15\text{ L/min}\ \text{O}_2$.
  - Disengage pop-off valve during resuscitation if higher inspiratory pressure is required to achieve visible chest rise.
  - E-C clamp technique; 1-second inspiratory squeeze just until chest rises; **rescue breathing rate: 1 breath every 2–3 seconds (20–30 breaths/min)**.
- **Endotracheal Tube (ETT) Sizing & Depth (p. 46):**
  - `< 1 year`: $3.5\text{ mm}$ uncuffed.
  - `1–2 years`: $4.0\text{ mm}$ uncuffed.
  - `> 2 years`:
    - $\text{Uncuffed ID (mm)} = 4 + (\text{age in years} / 4)$
    - $\text{Cuffed ID (mm)} = 3.5 + (\text{age in years} / 4)$ (cuffed preferred; keep $0.5\text{ mm}$ smaller and larger ready).
  - $\text{Insertion depth (cm at lips/teeth)} = 3 \times \text{ETT ID (mm)}$ (or length-based resuscitation tape).
  - Confirm with bilateral breath sounds, absence of gastric insufflation sounds, waveform capnography or colorimetric $\text{CO}_2$ detector (after 6 breaths), and CXR (mid-trachea).
- **DOPE Troubleshooting for Sudden Deterioration in Intubated Patients (Table 2.4, p. 47 & p. 102):**
  - **D**isplacement (esophageal or right mainstem bronchus intubation, circuit disconnection)
  - **O**bstruction (secretions, blood, mucus plug, kinked ETT)
  - **P**neumothorax (tension pneumothorax → immediate needle decompression + thoracostomy)
  - **E**quipment failure (oxygen source empty/disconnected, ventilator malfunction, leak, pop-off valve active)

## Module 3: Recognition and Management of Shock (pp. 50–64)

### Pathophysiology & Severity (pp. 50–53)
- $\text{DO}_2 = \text{CO} \times \text{CaO}_2$, where $\text{CO} = \text{HR} \times \text{SV}$ (preload, contractility, afterload).
- **Compensated Shock:** Clinical signs of poor perfusion (tachycardia, delayed CRT $> 2\text{ s}$, cool/mottled skin, oliguria) with **normal systolic BP**.
- **Hypotensive (Uncompensated) Shock:** Late sign indicating impending cardiac arrest ($\text{SBP} < 70 + 2 \times \text{age in years}$ for ages 1–10 y).

### Fluid & Vasoactive Management by Shock Type (pp. 58–64, Fig. 3.3–3.5)
- **Hypovolemic / Distributive / Obstructive Shock:** Isotonic crystalloid (NS or RL) bolus **$20\text{ mL/kg}$ over 5–10 minutes**; repeat $2\text{–}3\times$ ($40\text{–}60\text{ mL/kg}$) as needed based on perfusion and reassessing for volume overload (crackles, hepatomegaly).
- **Cardiogenic Shock / Myocardial Dysfunction:** Smaller fluid bolus **$5\text{–}10\text{ mL/kg}$ over 10–20 minutes**; early vasoactive/inotropic infusion (epinephrine, dopamine, or milrinone) and expert cardiology consultation.
- **Fluid-Refractory Septic Shock (p. 60, Fig. 3.4):**
  - Cold shock (cold extremities, delayed CRT, low BP): **Epinephrine** infusion.
  - Warm shock (warm extremities, flash CRT, wide pulse pressure / low BP): **Norepinephrine** infusion.
- **Anaphylactic Shock (p. 62):** IM epinephrine first-line, $20\text{ mL/kg}$ isotonic crystalloid bolus, albuterol for bronchospasm, antihistamine, corticosteroid, epinephrine infusion if refractory.
- **Obstructive Shock Causes (pp. 62–64):**
  - Tension pneumothorax → immediate needle decompression + chest tube.
  - Cardiac tamponade → pericardiocentesis + $20\text{ mL/kg}$ bolus bridge.
  - Ductal-dependent congenital heart lesion (neonate) → Prostaglandin $\text{E}_1$ ($\text{PGE}_1$) infusion + cardiology consult.
  - Massive pulmonary embolism → $20\text{ mL/kg}$ bolus, consider thrombolytics/anticoagulation.

## Module 4: Arrhythmias and Cardiac Arrest — Identification and Management (pp. 66–109)

### Chapter 2: Bradycardia with a Pulse (pp. 70–76, Fig. 4.2.7)
- **First-line:** Maintain patent airway, assist ventilation with 100% $\text{O}_2$ / BVM, attach monitor/defibrillator.
- **HR $< 60\text{/min}$ with poor perfusion despite adequate oxygenation & ventilation:** **Start chest compressions and ventilations immediately**; reassess after 2 minutes.
- **Epinephrine (Table 4.2.2, p. 75):**
  - **IV/IO:** $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:10,000$ / $0.1\text{ mg/mL}$ solution) every 3–5 minutes; **maximum IV/IO single dose $1\text{ mg}$**.
  - **ET (if no vascular access):** $0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $1:1,000$), maximum ET dose $2.5\text{ mg}$.
- **Atropine Sulfate (Table 4.2.3, p. 75) — for increased vagal tone, primary AV block, or cholinergic toxicity:**
  - **IV/IO:** $0.02\text{ mg/kg}$ push (may repeat once); **minimum single dose $0.1\text{ mg}$, maximum single dose $0.5\text{ mg}$**.
  - **ET:** $0.04\text{–}0.06\text{ mg/kg}$.
- **Emergency transcutaneous pacing (p. 75 & p. 109):** Consider for complete AV block or bradycardia refractory to oxygenation, ventilation, CPR, and pharmacotherapy.

### Chapter 3: Tachyarrhythmias with a Pulse (pp. 77–85, Fig. 4.3.9–4.3.10)
- **Narrow QRS ($\le 0.09\text{ s}$) — Sinus Tachycardia vs. SVT (Table 4.3.1, p. 80):**
  - **Sinus Tachycardia:** HR usually $< 220\text{/min}$ (infant) or $< 180\text{/min}$ (child), gradual onset, variable rate, P waves present → treat underlying cause.
  - **SVT:** HR usually $> 220\text{/min}$ (infant) or $> 180\text{/min}$ (child), abrupt onset, fixed rate, P waves absent/abnormal:
    - Stable SVT: Vagal maneuvers (ice to face in infants, blowing through occluded straw in children) → **Adenosine** rapid IV/IO push + rapid saline flush via 3-way stopcock ($0.1\text{ mg/kg}$ first dose [max $6\text{ mg}$], $0.2\text{ mg/kg}$ second dose [max $12\text{ mg}$]).
    - Unstable SVT: **Synchronized cardioversion $0.5\text{–}1\text{ J/kg}$**, increase to **$2\text{ J/kg}$** if needed (may give adenosine first only if IV/IO is immediately ready and does not delay cardioversion).
- **Wide QRS ($> 0.09\text{ s}$) — Ventricular Tachycardia (VT) / Aberrant SVT (pp. 80–84):**
  - Stable: Expert cardiology consultation; consider adenosine if regular monomorphic rhythm; pharmacologic cardioversion (amiodarone or procainamide).
  - Unstable: Immediate **synchronized cardioversion $0.5\text{–}1\text{ J/kg}$** (subsequent **$2\text{ J/kg}$**).

### Chapters 4 & 5: Pediatric Cardiac Arrest (Shockable & Non-Shockable) (pp. 86–101, Fig. 4.5.1)

#### High-Quality Pediatric CPR Parameters (Table 4.4.3, pp. 94–95)
- **Compression Rate:** $100\text{–}120\text{/min}$ (all pediatric ages).
- **Compression Depth (Table 4.4.3, p. 95):**
  - **Infant ($< 1\text{ year}$):** About $1.5\text{ inches}$ ($\approx 4\text{ cm}$) or $\frac{1}{3}$ anteroposterior chest diameter.
  - **Child ($1\text{ year}$ to puberty):** About $2\text{ inches}$ ($\approx 5\text{ cm}$) or $\frac{1}{3}$ anteroposterior chest diameter.
  - **Adolescent (post-puberty):** At least $2\text{ inches}$ ($5\text{–}6\text{ cm}$).
- **Compression-to-Ventilation Ratio (without advanced airway, p. 95):**
  - **2 or more rescuers:** $15:2$ with $100\%\ \text{O}_2$.
  - **Single rescuer:** $30:2$.
- **With Advanced Airway in Place (p. 95 & Module 2 p. 45):**
  - Continuous chest compressions without pauses for breaths.
  - Module 4 Ch. 4 p. 95 states $1\text{ breath every }6\text{–}8\text{ s}$ ($8\text{–}10\text{/min}$); Module 2 p. 45 / AHA 2020–2023 PALS uses $1\text{ breath every }2\text{–}3\text{ s}$ ($20\text{–}30\text{/min}$).
- **Physiologic Monitoring During CPR (p. 94 & p. 100):**
  - Full chest recoil between compressions; switch compressors every 2 minutes.
  - Target $\text{ETCO}_2\ 15\text{–}20\text{ mmHg}$ on waveform capnography (an $\text{ETCO}_2 < 10\text{ mmHg}$ after 20 minutes of CPR is a poor prognostic indicator).

#### Defibrillation Dosing & Pad Selection (p. 96 & Ch. 7 p. 107)
- **1st Shock:** $2\text{ J/kg}$
- **2nd Shock:** $4\text{ J/kg}$
- **3rd & Subsequent Shocks:** $4\text{–}10\text{ J/kg}$ maximum, or adult dose ($\le 200\text{ J}$ biphasic).
- **Pads/Paddles:** Infant pads for $< 10\text{ kg}$ ($< 1\text{ year}$); adult pads for $> 10\text{ kg}$ ($\ge 1\text{ year}$). Anteroposterior placement in infants and young children.

#### Arrest Medication Dosing & Administration (p. 75, p. 96, p. 99)
- **Flush Rule (p. 96):** Administer all IV/IO arrest drugs by rapid IV/IO push during compressions, followed by a **$5\text{–}10\text{ mL}$ normal saline bolus**.
- **Epinephrine:** $0.01\text{ mg/kg}$ ($0.1\text{ mL/kg}$ of $0.1\text{ mg/mL}$) IV/IO every 3–5 minutes (max $1\text{ mg}$).
  - **Non-shockable (Asystole / PEA):** Give as soon as possible, then every 3–5 minutes.
  - **Shockable (VF / pVT):** Give after the 2nd shock, then every 3–5 minutes.
- **Amiodarone:** $5\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO bolus (max $300\text{ mg}$/dose; max cumulative $15\text{ mg/kg}$) for refractory VF/pVT (after 3 shocks).
- **Lidocaine:** $1\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO loading dose (max $100\text{ mg}$) for refractory VF/pVT.
- **Magnesium Sulfate:** $25\text{–}50\text{ mg/kg}$ IV/IO (max $2\text{ g}$) for **Torsades de pointes** or hypomagnesemia.

#### Reversible Causes — H's and T's (Table 4.4.1 pp. 88–89 & Table 4.4.5 p. 97)
- **H's:** Hypovolemia, Hypoxia (Hypoxemia), Hydrogen ion excess (acidosis — consider sodium bicarbonate if arrest $> 30\text{ min}$), Hyperkalemia ($> 5.5\text{ mEq/L}$), Hypokalemia ($< 3.5\text{ mEq/L}$), Hypothermia ($< 35\ ^\circ\text{C}$), Hyperglycemia, **Hypoglycemia** ($< 45\text{ mg/dL}$ neonates; $< 60\text{ mg/dL}$ infants/children).
- **T's:** Tamponade (cardiac), Tension pneumothorax, Thrombosis (pulmonary), Thrombosis (coronary), Toxin.

### Chapter 6: Post–Cardiac Arrest Care (pp. 102–105, Tables 4.6.1 & 4.6.3)
1. **Airway & Oxygenation:** Confirm ETT position (clinical + capnography + CXR mid-trachea; troubleshoot with **DOPE**); titrate $\text{SpO}_2$ to **$94\text{–}99\%$** (avoid hyperoxia).
2. **Ventilation:** Maintain normocarbia on capnography / blood gas (avoid hypocarbia/hyperventilation except briefly for impending cerebral herniation).
3. **Hemodynamics:** Continuous BP/ECG/urine output monitoring; treat hypotension ($\text{SBP} < 5\text{th centile}$ / age formula) aggressively with isotonic fluids and vasoactive/inotropic infusions to preserve cerebral perfusion pressure ($\text{CPP} = \text{MAP} - \text{ICP}$).
4. **Targeted Temperature Management:** Prevent and vigorously treat fever ($\ge 1\ ^\circ\text{C}$ rise worsens hypoxic-ischemic brain injury); targeted temperature management per protocol.
5. **Glycemic Control:** Treat hypoglycemia ($< 45\text{ mg/dL}$ neonates; $< 60\text{ mg/dL}$ children) and avoid hyperglycemia.
6. **Neuroprotection & Diagnostics:** Optimize sedation, monitor EEG and treat seizures with anticonvulsants; obtain 12-lead ECG, ABG/VBG, lactate, electrolytes, calcium, CBC, CXR; transfer to PICU and conduct team debriefing.
